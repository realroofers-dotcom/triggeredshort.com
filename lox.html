    How this works

    Signing in

    Username and password. You stay signed in for 12 hours or until you close the tab.

    Your password is sent in a hidden header, never in the address bar. Nobody can look it up — not even the owner. It can only be replaced. If you forget it, ask an admin to set a new one.

    The crawl and what it is doing

    It reads every public company on the US exchanges — 10,398 of them — and records where each is incorporated, whether it was ever a shell, and how many times it changed its name.

    It reads the oldest registrants first, so early numbers lean toward Delaware and will shift as it finishes.

    Do not quote a percentage until the bar reads 100%.
    Site values — auto and manual

    auto is calculated from the database and refreshed every minute. manual was typed by a person and is never overwritten.

    Editing an auto value turns it manual. It stops updating from then on. Sometimes that is what you want. Usually it is not.

    Type in the box and press Save. Use Add a value for something new.

    Watchlist

    The companies followed closely — every filing, every name change.

    To add one you need its CIK, the SEC's number for that company. Search the company on sec.gov to find it. Paste the number, give it a name, press Add.

    Switching a company off stops the tracking and keeps everything already collected.

    Blank is not zero

    Where a figure shows blank or unknown, the company did not report it. That is not the same as the answer being none.

    Never read a blank as a clean record.
    If something goes wrong
    • Sign in failed — wrong username or password.
    • This account is switched off — an admin disabled it.
    • Not permitted — your role does not allow that action.
    • Nothing loads — your session expired. Sign in again.

    For admins

    Managing people

    Add someone with a username, name, role and a password of at least 8 characters.

    Switch off ends their session immediately — mid-click, not at the end of the day. Their password is kept, so switching them back on works unchanged.

    Delete removes the account. There is no undo.

    You cannot switch off, demote or delete your own account. That is deliberate: it stops the last admin from locking everyone out.

    The master key

    One key always works and is always admin. It is the way back in if every account is lost.

    Keep it somewhere other than this system. Anyone holding it has full control. Rotate it if it is ever seen — a screenshot counts as seen.
    Publishing

    Publish is admin-only and is not delegated. Editors draft, admins publish. Everything goes out under one byline, and the person whose name it is decides what carries it.

    What this is not

    An obscure page name and a password cut down noise. They are not a lock.

    The real lock is Cloudflare Access in front of the worker — an allow-list of email addresses with a one-time code. Until that is switched on, the key and these passwords are the only thing between this system and anyone who finds the page.
