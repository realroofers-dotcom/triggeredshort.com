The mechanism

How it can work

This is a sequence the documents permit. Every step below is provided for in ordinary warrant financing paperwork, and every step leaves a mark in a public record. Nothing here says any company or any investor has done this. It is drawn so a reader can recognise the shape if they ever see it.

The sequence

1

The paper is written

A company raises money by selling shares together with warrants. The warrant lets the holder buy more shares later at a set price. The terms that matter are in the exhibit, not in the announcement.

Shows up as 8-K Item 1.01 and 3.02 · the agreement at Exhibit 10.1 · the warrant at Exhibit 4.1

↓
2

The shares are registered

A registration statement makes those shares sellable. The fee table separates shares sold in the placement from shares underlying the warrants — and the effective date is the day the door opens.

Shows up as S-1 or S-3 · the fee table · EFFECT notice · 424B prospectus

↓
3

The stock runs

News, a partnership, a sector move, a crowd. The reason does not matter. What matters is that the warrants, struck above the market, are suddenly worth exercising.

Shows up as price and volume — often with no filing behind it

↓
4

The warrants are exercised into the strength

The holder converts paper into shares while the price is high. Where the warrant allows cashless exercise, the company receives no money for them.

Shows up as the share count on the next 10-Q or 10-K cover — a jump with no matching cash in the financing line

↓
5

The shares meet the buyers

The new shares are sold into the same crowd that drove the run. Volume runs far above normal while the price falls.

Shows up as volume against the 20-day average · the tape from pre-market to close

↓
6

The position is closed lower

The price ends where it began, or below it. The holder still has whatever warrants were not exercised — struck lower, if the terms reset — and the next run can be worked the same way.

Shows up as the price · FINRA short interest, which names nobody · the next 8-K

What it looks like on a chart

Green the riseRed the fall Bars volume — heaviest on the way down, not the way up

The tell is not the fall. Stocks fall. The tell is where the volume sits — heavier on the decline than on the rise, which means somebody had a great deal of stock to sell into it.

What the public record can and cannot show

Can be shown from public recordsCannot
The warrant terms — reset, cashless exercise, ownership blocker, and their section numbers Who exercised, and on which day
Shares registered, split between placement and warrant shares Who sold, and at what price
The date the registration went effective Who was short, and when they covered
Share count on each cover page, and the jumps between them Any intent behind any of it
Cash received in the financing line — or the absence of it
Price and volume, pre-market through close, every day
FINRA short interest, which reports a total and names nobody
Where the ownership blocker fits Many of these warrants cap the holder below five percent at any moment — often 4.99%, raisable on sixty-one days' notice. A holder can therefore have paper convertible into a great deal of a company and never appear in an ownership filing at all, because ownership rules measure the moment and the contract is built around the moment.
Why this page exists Every step above is ordinary and lawful. Warrants are a normal way to raise money, exercising them is the point of holding them, and selling shares you own is not wrongdoing. What we track

For every company on our list we record the sequence as it happens — the terms, the registration and its effective date, each change in the share count, and what the price and volume did around it. The findings are dated when we publish them and the outcome is recorded when it lands, including where nothing happened at all.
